**The walkthrough contradicts itself on days.** On Day 1, Sara commits a promise "for tomorrow" (Day 2), and the reminder fires at 8pm on Day 2. That evening she's asked "Did you cook instead of ordering **yesterday**?" But she made no promise for Day 1, so on Day 2 there's nothing to report on. If we keep "report on yesterday" and "commit for tomorrow", today's promise is always one that was made two check-ins earlier. Round 1 fixes this first, because everything else depends on it.

---

❓ **Q1** - **What does one check-in cover?**

- **A. Evening close-out.** Each promise is for one Kept day. At the check-in you report on the promise that's due, then commit the next one. The new promise is for whichever is later: the day after the promise you just reported, or today. When you check in on time, that means you report on today and commit for tomorrow. The walkthrough stays the same except one word: "yesterday" becomes "**today**" in the 8pm check-in. The question's wording comes from the promise's date, so a late report says "yesterday" or "on Tuesday". A nice side effect is that a user can never have more than one unreported promise, because each check-in creates exactly one.
- **B. Morning look-back.** Report on yesterday, commit for today. A report is never premature, but the reminder moves to the morning, and a late check-in makes that day's promise pointless.
- **C. As the walkthrough is written.** Report on yesterday, commit for tomorrow. This keeps the gap described above.

➡️ A. The report happens close to when you acted, the 8pm reminder still works, and the "whichever is later" rule handles late check-ins without creating a backlog. You can report any time on the promise's day, with no clock gate. If a tester taps Yes at 9am, we take their word for it.

---

❓ **Q2** - **Whose time zone?**

- Each user gets one time zone. We read it from the browser at sign-up and store it on the user, falling back to `Europe/London`.
- It is never updated automatically, so travelling doesn't shift their days.
- The other option is to hard-code `Europe/London` for everyone.

➡️ Store it per user from the browser, with `Europe/London` as the fallback. It costs one column and one hidden field. The `.ics` reminders in the should tier need a time zone anyway, so this leaves room for them.

---

❓ **Q3** - **When does the day roll over?**

- A Kept day runs from **4am to 4am** local time, not from midnight.
- A check-in at 00:30 still counts as the evening before. So "Did you cook today?" stays correct, and the new promise lands on the right "tomorrow".

➡️ Yes, 4am is the rollover. It's a single constant, and it's the same cutoff Anki uses for the same reason.

---

❓ **Q4** - **How long can a missed promise still be reported?**

A promise stays open for a report until the end of the Kept day after its own. In practice that means it can be reported on "today" or "yesterday".

- After that, the next check-in closes it as **Unreported**. It counts as neither kept nor slipped, and the adaptive engine ignores it.
- So a tester who disappears for a week isn't asked "Did you cook last Tuesday?" when they come back, and an absence doesn't shrink their promises.
- Nothing runs on a timer: the promise is closed when they next check in.
- The alternative is to treat an unreported promise as slipped. That's a stricter ledger, but promises would shrink while someone is on holiday.

➡️ Yes: a one-day grace period, then **Unreported**, as a third outcome.

---

❓ **Q5** - **What happens on a second visit in the same Kept day?**

Once the report is in and the next promise is committed, the screen shows a "done for today" state: their report, the promise ahead, and the learn card.

- They can change today's report until 4am. Each change is saved, and the latest one counts, which fits the event ledger later.
- In the must tier, a promise can't be changed once it's committed.

➡️ Yes: they can correct the report the same day, and the promise is locked.

---

❓ **Q6** - **When is the first promise, made at setup, for?**

There's no report at setup, so the rule from Q1 would make it a promise for **today**. The walkthrough says **tomorrow**, and the 8pm reminder fires the next day.

- Setup could be at 11pm, and it already takes 5 minutes.
- So the first promise is the one exception to the rule, and is always for tomorrow.

➡️ Yes: setup always commits for tomorrow.

❓ **Q7** - **What does `date` mean at the seam with Abdelmalek?**

❓ **Q8** - **How are days stored?**

- A promise stores its Kept day as a plain date (`@db.Date`), not as a timestamp.
- The user stores their time zone.
- A report stores when it was made (`timestamptz`) and belongs to exactly one promise.
- Because each check-in creates exactly one new promise, a user has at most one open promise at a time.

➡️ Yes. The details of the full tables stay in the shared-tables ticket.
