The switch is technically small, but I wouldn't replace Gemini outright with `openrouter/free`. It picks a random free model on each request, and that causes more trouble than the code change does.

## What I found
- **Code to change:** there's one AI call site, `generate-plan.ts`, behind [google.ts](src/server/ai/google.ts). A switch also touches `ai-usage/usage.ts`, `models.ts`, the env vars, the e2e fake and the eval script. The docs and copy that name Gemini would change too.
- **Model pool:** the live list has 14 named free models, and only 4 advertise structured outputs. One of those is a preview and one is a small 2.6B model. The planner's fixed plan shape depends on structured outputs.
- **Limits:** free models allow 20 requests a minute. The daily cap is 50 requests, or 1,000 once you've bought at least 10 credits.
- **Retries:** one person's setup can use up to 12 requests in the worst case: 2 builds × 2 planner attempts × 3 SDK tries. The docs don't say whether failed requests count against the cap.
- **OpenRouter's own guidance:** the router page pitches it for experimenting, prototyping and low-volume use, and warns of higher latency at peak times. Our client gives up on a request after 20 seconds, and the spec promises a plan in about 10.

## Round 1

❓ **Q1 - Replace or backstop**: I'm assuming you want this because Gemini's free tier is failing or hitting limits. If so, should Gemini stay as the first choice for plans, with the router used only when Gemini returns a 429 or 5xx? The alternatives are replacing Gemini everywhere, or pinning one specific `:free` model. If your reason is something else, such as quality or dropping Google, tell me, because that changes the answer.

➡️ Yes: keep Gemini first and use the router as the backstop for plans. Plan creation is what testers hit first, so it's where a fallback helps most. It also keeps today's plan quality whenever Gemini is up.

---

❓ **Q2 - Keep the Recall grader off the router**: Should the grader never use the router? A random model on each call means one person's answers could be marked by different models from one day to the next. The Grader check would then measure agreement with a changing mix of models, which undercuts the walkthrough's "Testable" claim. Issue #32 already specifies multiple choice when Gemini fails or is rate limited.

➡️ Yes: the grader stays on one fixed model and falls back to multiple choice, never the router.

---

❓ **Q3 - Privacy and tester consent**: Should we block free providers that may train on prompts, and tell testers before any goal goes through OpenRouter? They were told at sign-up that their goal and answers go to Google's Gemini, and [en.json:472](messages/en.json:472) says the same. Through the router, that data could reach whichever provider serves the model. OpenRouter offers `data_collection: "deny"` and a separate account setting for free models. The docs don't say how the block affects the router's pool, so it could shrink the pool or leave it empty.

➡️ Yes: set `data_collection: "deny"`, update the copy and message existing testers first. If a test request shows the pool is empty, we drop the router rather than relax the rule.

---

❓ **Q4 - Owner and timing**: Should you and Abdelmalek agree this today, and ship it before Tuesday's Nov 3 feature freeze or leave it out? He owns Learn, including the planner and the still-open grader issue #32, which specifies `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`. Testers are already live and the final demo is Thursday Nov 5, so a late change to the provider would be the riskiest thing in the build.

➡️ Yes: agree it with Abdelmalek today, and if it can't make the freeze, stay on Gemini through the demo.
